Hidden electronics / build manual · v5 · 2026-10-07

Build a hover-lit anniversary canvas.

Four foil sensors hide behind the painting. Bring a hand near a quadrant and its backlight fades up, stays lit for three seconds after release, then fades away. A hidden latching button selects either hover response or an always-on night-light mode.

01 / CONCEPT

What sits behind the painting

No mounting panel is required. Foil pads tape directly to the canvas back, while four cut-to-length COB strips, the controller, and the exposed wire harness attach to the wooden stretcher bars.

Rear frame layout / no insert

Why foil sensing

The ESP32-C3 has no built-in capacitive-touch peripheral. Each pad uses one GPIO and a 1MΩ resistor. Firmware charges the foil, releases it, and measures how long the voltage takes to fall. A nearby hand adds capacitance and stretches that time.

Why COB is the recommended light Chip-on-board strips mount many bare LED chips beneath one continuous phosphor coating, creating a seamless line instead of visible dots. That spreads light more evenly behind thin paint; discrete 5 mm LEDs tend to show hotspots. Heavy-body acrylic will still read mostly as a halo, and metallic-flake paint can reduce sensing range.

03 / PROVE IT FIRST

One-zone bench prototype

Do this before mounting anything to the painting. It proves the sensor, validates the target distance, and makes threshold tuning painless.

Prototype parts

  • ESP32-C3 SuperMini, USB-C data cable, Arduino IDE
  • Breadboard and jumper wires
  • One flat foil square, about 6 × 6 cm
  • One 1MΩ resistor
  • One white or warm-white LED and one 47–100Ω resistor
  • Cardboard, about 20 × 20 cm, as the canvas stand-in

IDE setup

Board: ESP32C3 Dev Module
USB CDC On Boot: Enabled
Serial: 115200 baud
Firmware: set DEBUG 1 and leave WHOLE_CANVAS_MODE false.

With DEBUG 1, the sketch waits up to two seconds for USB CDC before calibration so the first tuning lines are not lost. With debug off it starts immediately and does not block on an absent Serial Monitor.

Only pad 0 is wired for this prototype. Zones 1–3 may print FAULT or show erratic readings: a completely floating pin can decay through leakage within the 1 ms timeout and resemble a valid reading. Ignore those zones until their pads and 1 MΩ bleeds are connected.

GPIO0 ──┬── foil pad └──[1MΩ]── GND GPIO3 ──[47–100Ω]── white LED anode ── LED cathode ── GND

  1. Make the pad

    Tape the foil flat to the cardboard. Strip about 1 cm of a jumper, lay the bare end on the foil, and tape it firmly. A wire soldered to a folded foil tab is the sturdier final method.

  2. Wire one sensor and one LED

    Put the 1MΩ resistor close to the C3. The prototype LED is direct-drive only; the final four-zone harness uses an NPN transistor per zone.

  3. Calibrate with hands away

    Flash the firmware. The board prints a baseline and sweeps the LED once. Open Serial Monitor and note the idle value.

  4. Test fast and slow approaches

    Move a hand to 2–4 cm from the foil both quickly and gradually over 1–2 seconds. The reading should rise above baseline, the state should become HOVER, and the LED should fade up without the baseline following the hand.

  5. Simulate the canvas

    Place the cardboard between hand and foil. Pull away: the three-second afterglow begins when the sensor stops seeing the hand, then the light fades out over about 0.9 seconds.

Go / no-go gate Do not mount the final four-zone build until idle is stable, a hand triggers reliably through cardboard at the intended distance, and the rear side does not false-trigger. If it is flaky, fix pad size, wire length, or thresholds here.
04 / PANEL-FREE BUILD

Mount it directly to the frame

Use the canvas and wooden stretcher bars as the structure. The foil pads tape flat to the canvas back; COB segments, controller, and exposed wires stay on the bars so no hard part presses into the painting.

Parts

Everything needed is in the bill of materials, with quantities, qualified electrical values, and buy-versus-own status. Buy only the COB reel and the hookup wire.

COB strip preparation Cut only at the reel’s marked cut lines. Make one segment per quadrant, roughly 20 cm each, then solder hookup wire to the exposed copper pads. Keep one spare segment while fitting the frame.
Power budget At the selected listing’s roughly 8 W/m figure, four 20 cm segments total about 6.4 W—or 1.28 A from 5 V—when every zone is fully lit. A confirmed load at that level fits a 2 A output; prefer 5 V / 3 A if the cost difference is trivial, and do not use a laptop USB port. Meter the actual reel before final sizing.

Fallback: the 5 mm LEDs

If the COB strip cannot be sourced, two 5 mm LEDs per zone can work. Use 220 Ω per LED by default; 150 Ω is acceptable if brightness is insufficient. Expect more visible hotspots through thin paint.

  1. Mark four zones

    Lay the canvas face-down on a clean towel. Mark four quadrants lightly on the canvas back. Keep clear of stretcher hardware and leave a gap between pads.

  2. Tape four foil pads to the canvas

    Make four smooth foil squares. Tape each one flat directly to the canvas back. Tape a stripped wire end firmly onto each pad, or solder the wire to a folded foil tab before attaching it.

  3. Route the sensor wires

    Run each pad wire straight to the nearest stretcher bar, then along the wood to the C3. Tape it down every few centimetres and keep it away from LED power wires. Put each 1MΩ resistor near the board.

  4. Mount the mode button

    Put the 6-pin DPDT latching button where it can be reached from behind or below the frame. With a continuity meter, identify one pole’s common and the throw that closes when the button is latched in. Wire those two pins between GPIO7 and GND; leave the other pole unused. No external resistor is required.

  5. Cut and wire four COB segments

    Cut only on the strip’s marked lines, making one roughly 20 cm segment per quadrant. Feed the strip rail through the hold-current-rated PTC fuse. Solder each positive pad to that +5 V rail and each negative pad to its 2N2222 collector; each transistor switches one segment on the low side. Add a 10 kΩ base-to-GND pulldown per transistor, place the 470–1000 µF electrolytic across the strip power entry, and add 100 nF near the transistor/logic board. Build the four channels on perfboard, insulate every bare joint, and secure the harness to a stretcher bar.

  6. Prototype the optics, then mount

    Before committing, temporarily position one COB segment and test it through the painted canvas. COB light is directional; distance from the canvas, paint thickness, strip orientation, bar depth, and diffuser placement all change the result. Once the arrangement works, fix each segment to a stretcher bar and tape parchment across the frame opening, leaving air around the LEDs and keeping opaque tape off the phosphor surface.

  7. Add an optional rear shield

    If rear-side false triggers are a problem, tape one large foil sheet across the back opening and connect it to GND. Keep it fully separated from every pad, joint, and wire. Skip it if sensing is already stable.

  8. Calibrate in final position

    With the painting upright and hands away, power-cycle the C3 so it learns the installed baseline. Trigger every quadrant, check for crosstalk, and add tape anywhere a wire can move.

The selected strip is specified by its seller as including current limiting. Switch its negative lead through the NPN; do not add a series resistor unless the exact strip’s construction requires it. Bare 5 mm LEDs are different and each need their own series resistor.

A 1 kΩ base resistor supplies only about 2.5 mA, too little to saturate a 2N2222 at the segment’s roughly 320 mA load. Use 150 Ω for about 16.7 mA of base drive (forced β ≈ 19.2), or 180 Ω for about 14 mA (forced β ≈ 22.9). The datasheet’s 0.3 V and 1.0 V VCE(sat) limits are specified at forced β = 10, so expect somewhat more drop here and meter the assembled channel. If the drop is excessive, 120 Ω is a middle ground at about 21 mA and forced β ≈ 15.4. Add a 10 kΩ base-to-GND pulldown so the strip stays off while the GPIO floats during reset and boot.

Per-zone hover pad

Place each 1MΩ resistor near the board, not at the foil. Flat foil and short wire reduce noise. Use one pole of the six-pin DPDT button; identify the common and the throw that close in the latched-in position, and leave the second pole unused.

Two USB runs, one ground Keep the ESP32-C3 on its own USB-C cable. Feed the strip’s +5 V rail from a separate 2 A-or-higher output through the hold-current-rated PTC fuse, then connect that output’s ground to ESP32 GND. A multi-port charger is fine only when its per-port and total ratings cover both runs.
Protect and stabilize the strip branch Put the 470–1000 µF electrolytic across +5 V and GND at the strip power entry, observing polarity, and place 100 nF near the transistor/logic board. The PTC may drop roughly 0.1–0.15 V, the 2N2222 roughly 0.3–0.5 V at this forced β, and the cable adds more loss, potentially leaving the strip near 4.3 V—below its quoted 4.5–5.5 V band. Meter the strip rail at startup and with all four zones fully lit. If it sags, replace each NPN channel with a logic-level N-MOSFET whose RDS(on) is low at 3.3 V VGS; that removes the NPN saturation drop and its roughly 15–17 mA GPIO base load per channel.
Board-variant check before soldering GPIO10 is exposed on common ESP32-C3 SuperMini boards and is used by the supplied sketch, but board clones vary. Confirm your board’s printed pinout. This map deliberately avoids GPIO2 (a boot-strapping pin that must read HIGH during the roughly 3 ms strapping window after reset), GPIO8 (onboard LED and strapping), GPIO9 (BOOT strapping), GPIO11–17 (in-package flash bus), and GPIO18/19 (native USB). GPIO6, GPIO7, and GPIO10 are ordinary GPIOs; pad 1 therefore uses GPIO6 instead of GPIO2, where its 1MΩ resistor to GND could prevent boot.
06 / FIRMWARE

Flash, tune, then leave it alone

The sketch charges all four pads together, measures decay in CPU cycles, and uses a 17-sample boxcar to average over approximately one 60 Hz mains period, reducing periodic mains-related variation. Asymmetric baseline tracking and hysteresis stabilize detection. Four 20 kHz, 11-bit LEDC channels use gamma-corrected fades. Faulted pads are skipped and retried once per second. The latching switch directly selects the lighting mode; nothing is stored in flash.

/*
 * Canvas Glow — hover-activated backlight for a stretched-canvas painting
 *
 * 4 hover zones behind the canvas. A hand hovering near a zone fades that
 * zone's LEDs up to a steady, diffuse glow; it stays lit for a few seconds
 * after the hand leaves, then fades back out.
 * Set WHOLE_CANVAS_MODE to true and any hover lights the whole painting.
 *
 * Night-light mode: a latching on-off pushbutton (6-pin DPDT, one pole wired
 * GPIO 7 to GND) selects the mode mechanically — latched in is always-on
 * night light (all zones at full brightness), latched out is hover mode.
 * The firmware reads the pin directly every loop (INPUT_PULLUP, closed = LOW
 * = night-light on), so there is no toggle logic and nothing is stored in
 * flash: the mode is always whatever the switch is set to, which naturally
 * survives power cycles.
 *
 * Sensing: single-pin RC capacitive sensing. Each foil pad hangs off its own
 * GPIO with a 1M bleed resistor to GND. All four pads are charged together,
 * released, and each pad's decay is timed in CPU cycles: a nearby hand adds
 * capacitance and slows the fall. Readings are boxcar-filtered over ~17 ms
 * to average over approximately one 60 Hz mains period (Toronto), reducing
 * periodic mains-related variation. Faulted pads are skipped and retried
 * once per second.
 *
 * LEDs: 11-bit LEDC PWM at 20 kHz with gamma 2.2 correction. If the NPN
 * smears the low end of fades, drop PWM_FREQ to ~5000.
 *
 * Board: ESP32-C3 SuperMini (PlatformIO: esp32-c3-devkitm-1, USB CDC on boot,
 * 160 MHz, -O2)
 *
 * Wiring:
 *   LED zones (via NPN transistor per zone): GPIO 3, 4, 5, 10
 *   Hover pads (pad + 1M to GND each):       GPIO 0, 6, 20, 21
 *   Mode pushbutton (latching, 6-pin DPDT):  GPIO 7 (INPUT_PULLUP,
 *     one pole GPIO 7-GND, closed = LOW = night-light on)
 *   Avoid GPIO 2 (boot strap), 8 (onboard LED/strap), 9 (BOOT strap),
 *   11-17 (in-package flash bus), 18/19 (USB).
 *
 * Design: simultaneous pad sampling timed in CPU cycles, 17-sample boxcar
 * filter, 11-bit gamma-corrected LEDC, fault retry with shorted-pad
 * detection, 60 s stuck-zone recovery; latching mode button read directly
 * each loop (no NVS, no toggle/debounce). Baseline is Q12 fixed-point in
 * int32_t (worst case 160k cycles << 12 ≈ 655M, safely under 2.1G).
 */

unsigned long lastTriggerMs[4];
unsigned long hoverStartMs[4];

// Charge all pads in mask, release, timestamp each pad's fall in CPU cycles.
// out[z] = decay cycles, or -1 if not measured / never fell (fault).
// The critical section lasts only until the slowest pad falls (typically
// 20-100 µs); the full timeout applies only to faulted pads, which are then
// skipped and retried once per PAD_RETRY_MS.
void IRAM_ATTR readPads(uint32_t mask, int32_t out[4])
{
    uint32_t fallAt[4] = { 0, 0, 0, 0 };
    uint32_t pending = mask;

    portENTER_CRITICAL(&padMux);
    REG_WRITE(GPIO_OUT_W1TS_REG, mask);
    REG_WRITE(GPIO_ENABLE_W1TS_REG, mask);
    uint32_t t0 = ESP.getCycleCount();
    while (ESP.getCycleCount() - t0 < chargeCycles) { }
    REG_WRITE(GPIO_ENABLE_W1TC_REG, mask);
    t0 = ESP.getCycleCount();
    uint32_t dt;
    do {
        uint32_t fell = pending & ~REG_READ(GPIO_IN_REG);
        dt = ESP.getCycleCount() - t0;
        if (fell) {
            for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z)
                if (fell & padBit[z])
                    fallAt[z] = dt;
            pending &= ~fell;
        }
    } while (pending && dt < timeoutCycles);
    portEXIT_CRITICAL(&padMux);

    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z)
        out[z] = ((mask & ~pending) & padBit[z]) ? (int32_t)fallAt[z] : -1;
}

int32_t filterPush(uint8_t z, int32_t s)
{
    int32_t& slot = ring[z][ringIdx[z]];
    ringSum[z] += s - slot;
    slot = s;
    if (++ringIdx[z] == FILTER_LEN)
        ringIdx[z] = 0;
    return ringSum[z] / FILTER_LEN;
}

void ledInit(uint8_t z)
{
#if ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION_MAJOR >= 3
    bool ok = ledcAttach(LED_PINS[z], PWM_FREQ, PWM_BITS);
#else
    bool ok = ledcSetup(z, PWM_FREQ, PWM_BITS) != 0;
#endif
    if (!ok)
        Serial.printf("ERROR: LEDC setup failed for zone %d (GPIO %d)\n", z, LED_PINS[z]);
#if ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION_MAJOR >= 3
    ledcWrite(LED_PINS[z], 0);
#else
    ledcAttachPin(LED_PINS[z], z);
    ledcWrite(z, 0);
#endif
}

void ledWrite(uint8_t z, uint32_t d)
{
#if ESP_ARDUINO_VERSION_MAJOR >= 3
    ledcWrite(LED_PINS[z], d);
#else
    ledcWrite(z, d);
#endif
}

long cyclesToTenthsUs(int32_t c)
{
    return (long)c * 10 / (long)cpuMhz;
}

void setup()
{
    Serial.begin(115200);
#if ARDUINO_USB_MODE && ARDUINO_USB_CDC_ON_BOOT
    Serial.setTxTimeoutMs(0); // never block when no USB host is reading
#endif
#if DEBUG
    while (!Serial && millis() < 2000) { }
#endif

    cpuMhz = getCpuFrequencyMhz();
    chargeCycles = PAD_CHARGE_US * cpuMhz;
    timeoutCycles = PAD_TIMEOUT_US * cpuMhz;
    trigMarginCycles = (int32_t)(TRIG_MARGIN_US * cpuMhz);
    faultMinCycles = (int32_t)(FAULT_MIN_BASELINE_US * cpuMhz);

    pinMode(BUTTON_PIN, INPUT_PULLUP);
    bool startOn = (digitalRead(BUTTON_PIN) == LOW); // latching switch position

    const float maxDuty = (float)((1 << PWM_BITS) - 1);
    for (uint16_t i = 0; i < 256; ++i)
        gammaLut[i] = (uint16_t)lroundf(powf(i / 255.0f, GAMMA) * maxDuty);

    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        ledInit(z);
        // OUTPUT first routes the GPIO matrix to plain GPIO; INPUT leaves it high-Z, no pulls
        pinMode(PAD_PINS[z], OUTPUT);
        pinMode(PAD_PINS[z], INPUT);
        padBit[z] = 1UL << PAD_PINS[z];
        allPadsMask |= padBit[z];
    }

    Serial.println("Canvas Glow — calibrating (keep hands away from pads)...");
    int32_t sum[4] = { 0, 0, 0, 0 };
    uint16_t n[4] = { 0, 0, 0, 0 };
    for (uint16_t i = 0; i < 200; ++i) {
        int32_t s[4];
        readPads(allPadsMask, s);
        for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
            if (s[z] >= 0) {
                sum[z] += s[z];
                ++n[z];
            }
        }
        delay(1);
    }
    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        int32_t base = n[z] ? sum[z] / n[z] : (int32_t)(50 * cpuMhz);
        baseQ[z] = base << BASE_Q;
        lastV[z] = base;
        for (uint8_t i = 0; i < FILTER_LEN; ++i)
            ring[z][i] = base;
        ringSum[z] = base * FILTER_LEN;
        // No reading at all, or a baseline under ~2 µs (shorted pad): fault.
        padFaulted[z] = (n[z] == 0) || (base < faultMinCycles);
        padFaultMs[z] = millis();
        long b = cyclesToTenthsUs(base);
        Serial.printf("  zone %d baseline: %ld.%ld us%s\n", z, b / 10, b % 10,
            padFaulted[z] ? " (FAULT)" : "");
    }

    Serial.printf("Mode: %s (latching button on GPIO %d)\n",
        startOn ? "ALWAYS-ON night light" : "hover", BUTTON_PIN);

#if SELF_TEST
    Serial.println("Self-test: sweeping zones...");
    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        ledWrite(z, (1 << PWM_BITS) - 1);
        delay(400);
        ledWrite(z, 0);
        delay(200);
    }
#endif
    Serial.println("Ready — hover a hand over a pad.");
}

void loop()
{
    static TickType_t lastWake = xTaskGetTickCount();
    vTaskDelayUntil(&lastWake, pdMS_TO_TICKS(TICK_MS));
    const unsigned long now = millis();

    // ---- Mode: latching pushbutton, read directly every loop ----
    // Switch closed (GPIO 7 = LOW) = always-on night light; open = hover.
    // No toggle state, no debounce, nothing in flash — the mode is the switch.
    bool alwaysOn = (digitalRead(BUTTON_PIN) == LOW);

    // ---- Sense ----
    uint32_t mask = 0;
    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        if (padFaulted[z] && now - padFaultMs[z] >= PAD_RETRY_MS)
            padFaulted[z] = false;
        if (!padFaulted[z])
            mask |= padBit[z];
    }
    int32_t s[4];
    readPads(mask, s);

    bool anyHeld = false;
    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        if (s[z] >= 0 && s[z] < faultMinCycles) {
            // Shorted pad reads ~0: fault, not a valid reading.
            padFaulted[z] = true;
            padFaultMs[z] = now;
        } else if (s[z] >= 0) {
            int32_t v = filterPush(z, s[z]);
            lastV[z] = v;
            int32_t base = baseQ[z] >> BASE_Q;
            if (!hovering[z]) {
                // Asymmetric Q12 baseline tracking: fast down (64 ms), slow up
                // (~4 s), frozen while hovering, so a slow hand approach can't
                // drag the baseline up with it.
                int32_t vq = v << BASE_Q;
                baseQ[z] += (vq - baseQ[z]) >> (vq < baseQ[z] ? BASE_DOWN_SHIFT : BASE_UP_SHIFT);
                base = baseQ[z] >> BASE_Q;
                hovering[z] = v * 100 > base * TRIG_PCT && v > base + trigMarginCycles;
                if (hovering[z])
                    hoverStartMs[z] = now;
            } else if (v * 100 < base * REL_PCT) {
                hovering[z] = false;
            }
            if (hovering[z]) {
                lastTriggerMs[z] = now;
                everTriggered[z] = true;
                if (now - hoverStartMs[z] >= HOVER_RESET_MS) {
                    // Stuck-zone recovery for unattended operation: re-baseline
                    // to the present reading instead of latching until reboot.
                    baseQ[z] = lastV[z] << BASE_Q;
                    hovering[z] = false;
                }
            }
        } else if (mask & padBit[z]) {
            padFaulted[z] = true;
            padFaultMs[z] = now;
        }
        inHold[z] = everTriggered[z] && now - lastTriggerMs[z] < HOLD_MS;
        if (inHold[z])
            anyHeld = true;
    }

    // ---- Glow ----
    for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
        bool lit = alwaysOn || (WHOLE_CANVAS_MODE ? anyHeld : inHold[z]);
        uint16_t b = brightness[z];
        if (lit)
            b = (b > 65535 - FADE_UP_STEP) ? 65535 : b + FADE_UP_STEP;
        else
            b = (b < FADE_DOWN_STEP) ? 0 : b - FADE_DOWN_STEP;
        brightness[z] = b;
        uint16_t d = gammaLut[b >> 8];
        if (d != duty[z]) {
            duty[z] = d;
            ledWrite(z, d);
        }
    }

#if DEBUG
    static unsigned long lastDbg = 0;
    if (now - lastDbg >= 500) {
        lastDbg = now;
        char buf[256];
        int n = 0;
        for (uint8_t z = 0; z < 4; ++z) {
            long b = cyclesToTenthsUs(baseQ[z] >> BASE_Q);
            long v = cyclesToTenthsUs(lastV[z]);
            n += snprintf(buf + n, sizeof(buf) - n, "z%d base=%ld.%ld v=%ld.%ld %s  ",
                z, b / 10, b % 10, v / 10, v % 10,
                padFaulted[z] ? "FAULT" : hovering[z] ? "HOVER"
                    : inHold[z]                       ? "HOLD "
                                                      : "----");
        }
        snprintf(buf + n, sizeof(buf) - n, "mode=%s\n", alwaysOn ? "ALWAYS-ON" : "hover");
        Serial.print(buf);
    }
#endif
}
TRIG_PCT

Reach

Lower from 135 toward 125 if a real hover does not trigger. Too low can cause false positives.

TRIG_MARGIN_US

Noise floor

Raise above 12 if the zone flickers near idle. This requires an absolute rise as well as a ratio rise.

HOLD_MS

Afterglow

3000 means three seconds at full brightness after the hand leaves. Change to taste.

Always-on night light Latch the GPIO7 button in to connect the pin to GND: LOW selects always-on mode and all four zones fade to full brightness. Unlatch it to open the circuit: HIGH returns to hover mode. The firmware reads the switch directly every loop, with no toggle logic, debounce state, or flash storage. After any power cycle, the mode follows the physical switch position.
Timing and baseline limits The configured fade-up takes about 0.4 seconds and fade-down about 0.9 seconds at the nominal 1 ms task cadence; a pad timeout can stretch them slightly. Baseline adaptation falls quickly but rises with a roughly four-second time constant so a gradual approach is less likely to drag it upward. While hovering, adaptation freezes; after 60 seconds of continuous hover, the firmware re-baselines to the present reading and releases the zone so an unattended installation cannot stay latched indefinitely.

08 / DIAGNOSE

Fast fixes at the bench

Use serial output to separate sensing problems from LED or mechanical problems.

Zone never triggers
Make the pad larger, shorten the pad lead, confirm the 1MΩ resistor, or lower TRIG_PCT toward 125. Test both fast and slow approaches at the known-good prototype distance.
Zone flickers or false-triggers
Tape loose pad wires firmly to the stretcher bars, raise TRIG_MARGIN_US, route sensor wires away from LED wiring, and smooth the foil. If needed, add the optional grounded rear shield without letting it touch a pad or joint.
Zone stays on
Inspect for moved foil or wiring. Baseline adaptation freezes during hover, but after 60 seconds of continuous hover the firmware re-baselines to the present reading and releases the zone automatically. If it repeats, reboot with all hands away so startup calibration is clean. A missing 1 MΩ resistor instead causes the zone never to trigger.
Zone reports FAULT or stops updating
Inspect the pad lead and 1 MΩ resistor. A pad shorted to GND produces a near-zero reading; any baseline under about 2 µs is marked FAULT during calibration and retry. A disconnected or excessively capacitive pad instead times out: readPads() returns −1, marks it FAULT, skips it, and retries once per second. A timeout can hold the simultaneous read for up to 1 ms before the pad is skipped.
More than one quadrant triggers
Increase spacing between foil pads, reduce pad size, shorten and secure the leads, and add the optional grounded rear shield if necessary. Tune each quadrant through the same canvas stand-in used in the prototype.
COB zone is dim or dead
Check the segment’s +/− pads, transistor pin order, the 150 Ω or 180 Ω base resistor, its 10 kΩ pulldown, and the shared ground between the C3 and strip supply. Meter the strip rail at startup and under full load; if all zones sag, verify the strip output is rated for at least 2 A, preferably 3 A. For the 5 mm LED fallback, also check each 220 Ω series resistor.
Mode button does not switch modes
Confirm the latched-in position connects GPIO7 to GND and the released position opens it. If the behavior is reversed, move the wire from one throw to the other on the same DPDT pole. The second pole remains unused.
Board will not boot
Disconnect the external wiring, boot bare, then reconnect one zone at a time. Verify the actual SuperMini pinout and keep boot-strapping GPIO2/8/9 clear; pad 1 belongs on GPIO6. A pad or driver on the wrong board pin can interfere with startup.

Final install check

  • Each quadrant triggers only its own COB segment.
  • Hover through the painted canvas works at the proven distance.
  • Fade-up, three-second afterglow after release, and fade-out are smooth with no idle flicker.
  • The latching button selects all-on night-light mode, and after a USB power cycle the mode follows the switch position.
  • No hard component or bare joint touches or presses against the canvas.
  • The PTC fuse is in the strip’s +5 V feed, its warm derated hold current exceeds the measured normal load, and it has been tested warm; the bulk electrolytic is correctly polarized at the power entry; 100 nF local decoupling is fitted near the driver board.
  • Pads, COB segments, controller, and exposed insulated wires stay secure when the frame tilts.
  • Both USB cables exit cleanly; the strip supply is rated 2A or higher and all grounds are common.
